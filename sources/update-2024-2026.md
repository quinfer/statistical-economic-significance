### Menkveld: primary retrieval and forward-citation trail

The [publisher full text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13337) was retrieved. Read material comprises the abstract, introduction, selected parts of I.A and I.C, II.D and III. Complete methods, supplements and code were not audited. The long author list is explicitly abbreviated after the first nine authors; the publisher byline and Appendix A remain the authority for the complete list.

**Source claim:** The authors distinguish uncertainty arising from different analysis paths from sampling uncertainty and report substantial variation across analysts working with the same data. **Project interpretation:** this adds a reason to examine the stability of an economic magnitude across analytical choices. It does not establish that all paths are equally defensible or provide an automatic additive correction to a standard error.

**Forward-citation trail:** the introductory “Contribution to the Literature” explicitly cites Harvey and Liu (2020) and Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen (2023). The nearby statistical-framework discussion cites Harvey, Liu and Zhu (2016). These links were checked in retrieved text; no exhaustive citation-network coverage is claimed.

**Open audit question:** inspect section III's treatment of Bonferroni and dependence rather than copying its brief characterisation into teaching materials. Check reconciliation with the preregistered version in Appendix B. These issues remain unadjudicated.

Root reports metadata verification against the author page and selected reading of the dated author PDF, with an additional assigned reader. This is a **working paper**, not a verified journal publication. **Project interpretation:** the topic adds an economics-specific route for comparing practical thresholds with uncertainty; detailed findings should be drawn only from the recorded passages in its paper note.

The author's page/CV links [DOI 10.31222/osf.io/d7sqr_v2](https://doi.org/10.31222/osf.io/d7sqr_v2), but the resolver returned 403. Do not count the author PDF and repository deposit as separate contributions.

### Robust Bayes treatment choice: final article and earlier draft

Root retrieved [Cambridge's primary article HTML](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/econometric-theory/article/robust-bayes-treatment-choice-with-partialidentification/4125FE898E298193C00B107953D36ECC). The reported read material is the abstract, introduction, selected framework sections 2.1–2.3, section 3 Assumption 1, and the conclusion. Other proofs and detailed results have not been fully audited. The named authors are Aradillas Fernández, Montiel Olea, Qiu, Stoye and Tinda; the registry carries the verified author metadata.

**Project interpretation:** this contribution extends the decision comparison to partial identification and robust Bayesian criteria. Its distinctions between decision rules must be tied to the assumptions actually checked, rather than generalised to every decision under uncertainty.

The earlier [arXiv record 2408.11621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11621) was first submitted **21 August 2024** and revised **19 December 2025**. It is an earlier version of the same work, not an extra source. Cite the verified 2026 FirstView article for publication identity; do not invent a subsequent volume or issue.

### Armstrong, Kitagawa and Tetenov — priority comparative reading

**Exact reference:** Timothy B. Armstrong, Toru Kitagawa and Aleksey Tetenov (2026), “Statistical Decision Theory and Empirical Practice,” Journal of Political Economy Microeconomics 4(3), 1079–1104. DOI [10.1086/737237](https://doi.org/10.1086/737237).

**Primary verification:** [publisher record](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/737237), metadata, abstract and publication history directly retrieved. Published online **11 June 2026**; issue dated **1 August 2026**, both before cutoff. Type is “Special Article: Next Frontiers”; the abstract calls it a review article. Final full text was not obtained: the full-text URL redirected to the abstract/access page.

**Source claim — final abstract:** The authors explain how hypothesis testing, estimation and preanalysis plans can be understood within Wald's decision framework, and compare recent alternative interpretations of empirical practice.

**Separate version record:** [author-hosted draft](https://tetenov.com/decision_theory_review.pdf), dated **5 January 2025**, was successfully opened. Abstract and introduction, printed pp. 1–3, were read. It distinguishes lacking a decision-theoretic motivation from possessing one that is unsatisfactory. This draft detail must remain labelled draft-specific until checked against the final article.

**Disposition:** highest-priority next comparison with Manski; retained outside the core in this capped update. Do not base the present synthesis on unread detailed final results.

### Mitton — economic-magnitude reporting

**Exact reference:** Todd Mitton (2024), “Economic Significance in Corporate Finance,” The Review of Corporate Finance Studies 13(1), 38–79. DOI [10.1093/rcfs/cfac008](https://doi.org/10.1093/rcfs/cfac008).

**Primary verification:** [publisher record](https://academic.oup.com/rcfs/article-abstract/13/1/38/6537558), complete abstract and metadata returned through search. [Publisher issue contents](https://academic.oup.com/rcfs/issue/13/1) and [author CV](https://marriott.byu.edu/directory/details/vita?id=1065) corroborate final pagination and 2024 issue year. Direct article opening failed at the publisher redirect; detailed paper text was not read.

**Source claim — abstract:** Mitton advocates standard-deviation-scaled effect measures, sufficient reporting to reconstruct them and benchmarks for judging magnitude.

**Date distinction:** first online **26 February 2022**, corrected/typeset April 2022; February **2024** issue. It is a 2024 final issue citation, not evidence that this proposal first appeared in 2024.

**Disposition:** priority measurement/scaling reading outside the capped core. Its recommendations should be compared with raw economic units and welfare thresholds; the abstract does not resolve that comparison.

### Manganelli — judgment and testing in an allocation decision

**Exact reference:** Simone Manganelli (2025), “Statistical decision functions with judgment,” Journal of Economic Theory 223, article 105940. DOI [10.1016/j.jet.2024.105940](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jet.2024.105940).

**Primary verification:** [publisher article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022053124001467), metadata, abstract and selected introduction text retrieved via search; direct open returned 403. An [author-hosted published PDF](https://simonemanganelli.org/Simone/Research_files/SDFwJ_JET.pdf) was located with a first-page extract but not fully read. Earlier ECB Working Paper 2512 is dated 2021 and is not a second independent publication.

**Source claim — abstract:** The author embeds a judgmental decision in a testing rule and illustrates it with asset allocation. The significance level has a decision interpretation within the proposed framework.

**Disposition:** relevant counterexample to a categorical separation of testing and decisions; deferred to protect the capped update's coverage. Admissibility and optimality results require a full assumptions/proofs audit.
