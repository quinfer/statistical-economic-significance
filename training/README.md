# PhD training design brief

**Slide planning only in this directory. No slides or Quarto deck generated.** The [critical-review gate](../reviews/review-gate.md) remains closed. The [curriculum](../CURRICULUM.md), [Markdown exercises](../exercises/README.md) and [deterministic worked examples](../implementations/README.md) are now available as a separate learning pathway. Their publication does not authorise slides.

Intended outcomes are to distinguish magnitude, evidence and action; interpret uncertainty without significance shortcuts; justify an economic threshold; evaluate selected or noisy estimates; and defend a decision under stated costs and assumptions.

| Planned activity | Economics/finance setting | Methodological question |
|---|---|---|
| Same estimate, changing precision | Policy effect on annual earnings, with specified target population and programme cost | What changes in the magnitude claim when the standard error changes? |
| Same precision, changing consequences | Per-person benefit scaled across populations and fiscal costs | What makes a small effect matter, and which quantities are themselves uncertain? |
| Four outcomes of zero-null and equivalence tests | A policy effect measured in pounds, with a justified practical margin | Can an effect differ from zero yet be negligible? Can both tests be inconclusive? |
| Type S/M selection experiment | Noisy estimates of an investment signal | How does selecting significant estimates distort signs and sizes? |
| Decisions with asymmetric losses | A reversible pilot versus a costly irreversible rollout | Can actions differ while the data and p-value stay the same? |
| Predictive portfolio choice | Synthetic stock/cash allocation with estimated predictability | How do priors, risk aversion, parameter uncertainty and trading costs change action? |
| Multiple searches and out-of-sample checks | Synthetic factor research | How do false discoveries, missed discoveries and shrinkage interact? |
| Adversarial seminar | Matched passages in the 2008 exchange and later reform proposals | Which disagreement concerns evidence, which concerns aims, and what would settle each? |

All proposed data are synthetic unless a later exercise explicitly obtains and documents real data. The learning exercises now contain labelled synthetic calibrations and hand-checkable calculations, not empirical findings. Each exercise exposes assumptions and includes a case that challenges its apparent lesson. Monte Carlo experiments and slide development remain deferred.

The eventual deck should have progressive explanation, visible units, readable plots, references near claims, speaker notes identifying controversies, and discussion prompts that admit more than one defensible answer. Quarto reveal.js is the requested delivery format; use a reproducible rendering environment once authoring begins.
