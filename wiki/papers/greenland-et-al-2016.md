## Source claim

Inference depends on assumptions about data generation, analysis and selection. Confidence intervals help assess substantively important magnitudes but share assumptions and interpretation risks with tests. Their coverage concerns repeated use of a procedure, rather than a posterior probability for a realised interval. The authors distinguish prospective power from power calculated using observed effects and warn against treating interval boundaries as sharp truth boundaries.
