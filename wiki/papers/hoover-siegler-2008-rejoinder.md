## Source claim

The rejoinder defends uncertainty assessment while accepting the distinction between importance and precision. It questions whose losses should govern scientific evidence (p. 59), argues for model-based counterfactual inference (pp. 60–61), and restates survey objections (pp. 65–66). It expressly says the 2008 book was unavailable when these responses were written (p. 65).

## Project interpretation

Our interpretation: neither the noisy-estimate objection nor contextual loss arguments justify making conventional rejection a universal prerequisite for knowledge or action. The book and exchange require separate evidence trails.
