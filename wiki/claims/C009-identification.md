# C009: Precise estimation establishes economic relevance

**Status:** rejected as a universal proposition. **Type:** project interpretation.

Even a precise estimate needs a defensible target and identification argument before it can support a causal or policy claim. Relevance elsewhere additionally depends on the population and setting, and a large aggregate benefit may conceal adverse distributional effects.

## Evidence trail

[Deaton–Cartwright](../papers/deaton-cartwright-2018-rcts.md), selected discussions of precision and extrapolation, motivates the first distinction. [Manski 2021](../papers/manski-2021-econometrics-decisions.md), abstract, motivates examining model-based decisions across a broader feasible state space. Distributional and equilibrium extensions are project questions, not established findings from these consulted passages.

**Gap partly addressed:** [Aradillas Fernández et al. 2026](../papers/aradillas-fernandez-etal-2026-robust-bayes.md) adds a verified contemporary partial-identification treatment-choice framework. Selected reading covers its normal signal, linear mappings, convex symmetric parameter set and restrictive prior class (sections 2–3), plus its conclusion. Ex ante/ex post disagreement does not establish which criterion should govern policy. Proofs and complete applications remain unread. More observations need not resolve an identification gap.
