# C005: Abandon, tighten or retain thresholds

**Status:** contested. **Type:** competing normative and methodological proposals.

## Evidence trail

These are overlapping proposals along different axes: scientific labels, discovery standards and operational decision rules. They are not mutually exclusive camps.

| Proposal | Evidence consulted | Question for review |
|---|---|---|
| Remove categorical scientific labels; retain continuous information and possible specialised decision uses | [McShane et al.](../papers/mcshane-etal-2019-abandon.md), pp. 235–236; [Amrhein et al.](../papers/amrhein-greenland-mcshane-2019.md), pp. 306–307 | Does removal improve reasoning and selection incentives in practice? Amrhein et al. explicitly reject a p-value ban. |
| Tighten discovery standards | [Benjamin et al.](../papers/benjamin-et-al-2018.md), abstract | What are the costs of missed discoveries and extra data collection? |
| Retain useful tests while changing reporting | [Imbens](../papers/imbens-2021.md), abstract | Which questions genuinely warrant a null test? |

**Provenance distinction:** [ASA 2016](../papers/wasserstein-lazar-2016.md) is an official statement; [Wasserstein et al. 2019](../papers/wasserstein-schirm-lazar-2019.md) is an editorial expressing its authors' views.

**Project interpretation:** these proposals cannot be ranked without specifying goals and consequences. A scientific reporting convention, a publication filter and an operational decision threshold need not have the same justification. Avoid collapsing continuous p-value use into categorical testing.
