# C003: Non-significance and negligibility

**Status:** the blanket implication is rejected within the specified testing framework. **Type:** conceptual claim supported by consulted methods text.

Failure to reject an exact zero does not establish that an effect is economically negligible. Negligibility is a different proposition involving a justified practical margin and adequate evidence relative to that margin.

## Evidence trail

[Lakens 2017](../papers/lakens-2017-equivalence.md), introduction and Figure 1 discussion, separates the two testing questions. [Schuirmann](../papers/schuirmann-1987-tost.md), abstract, supplies the formal equivalence-testing anchor. [Lakens–Scheel–Isager](../papers/lakens-scheel-isager-2018.md), discussion, qualifies the exercise through margin justification and prespecification.

**Open methodological disagreement:** adopting a threshold-based equivalence procedure may be useful for a specified decision or falsification task while leaving objections to categorical scientific reporting unresolved. An average effect inside a margin need not imply negligible effects for every subgroup.

## Contemporary challenge and qualification

[Fitzgerald 2025](../papers/fitzgerald-2025-equivalence.md), a selected-section working-paper reading, brings this issue directly into economics. Its pp. 22 and 34 preserve distinctions between equivalence failure and large effects, and between generic audit margins and question-specific margins.

**Project conclusion:** an equivalence-testing failure rate does not by itself reveal an actual Type II error rate or the frequency of false null conclusions. A [synthetic exact-zero counterexample](../evidence/mathematical-checks.md) establishes that logical gap. The [adversarial audit](../disagreements/equivalence-audit.md) also distinguishes wide fixed margins from data-dependent selection, and symmetric negligibility from one-sided economic viability.
