# C004: Misuse of testing versus validity of testing

**Status:** documentary evidence improved; population prevalence and full historical verdict unresolved. **Type:** project interpretation.

An audit reporting widespread misuse does not alone establish that every use of testing is invalid. Conversely, a defensible testing procedure does not establish that economists use it well.

## Evidence trail

Selected original sections of [McCloskey–Ziliak 1996](../papers/mccloskey-ziliak-1996.md), the alternative EJW publication of [Ziliak–McCloskey 2004](../papers/ziliak-mccloskey-2004.md), and [the book](../papers/ziliak-mccloskey-2008-cult.md) have now been read directly. The [complete 2008 exchange](../disagreements/2008-exchange.md) provides the opposing interpretations. These reading depths do not mean the surveys have been independently recoded.

**Verified documentary observations:** the [coding audit](../evidence/aer-coding-audit.md) reproduces the original 137-paper matrix's approximately 82% adverse Q16 classifications, while finding unresolved discrepancies in printed tables, author totals and omission claims. The book reports a revised 187-paper survey and roughly 79% adverse Q16 classifications; that expanded matrix was not retrieved. The reporting-of-size item and decisiveness item measure different behaviours. Neither headline should become an unqualified claim that economists never discuss magnitudes.

**Project case assessment:** direct or explicitly identified draft evidence weakens several accusations of wholesale indifference to economic magnitudes. It also preserves narrower concerns about significance filtering, non-rejection and subgroup claims. The ledgers for [Darby/Woodbury–Spiegelman](../evidence/aer-darby-woodbury.md) and [Bernanke–Blinder/Bernheim–Wantz/Becker et al.](../evidence/aer-macro-finance-addiction.md) now link direct final-text checks for [Darby](../evidence/darby-final-followup.md) and [Bernheim–Wantz](../evidence/bernheim-wantz-final-followup.md). Their earlier draft-only access limits have been resolved; their economic conclusions are not thereby validated. These purposively selected cases cannot estimate the profession's error rate.

## Disagreements retained

The reply concedes omissions and weighting difficulties (p. 45). Hoover and Siegler concede that omissions need not change survey conclusions (*Sound and fury*, n. 3, pp. 31–32); their rejoinder treats omission as ancillary (p. 62). Thus correcting an omission list is not itself a refutation of the survey diagnosis. Its own Simon overlap and 81/82 count discrepancy nevertheless require an explanation.

The rejoinder says the book was unavailable at writing (p. 65). Our separate [book reading](../evidence/cult-2008-reading.md) now covers only pp. 57–61 and 74–85. It finds a qualified methodological programme and some unresolved numerical/construct tensions, not grounds for treating every chapter as reviewed.

**Evidence still needed:** a complete article crosswalk, original item codes and passage-level justifications, an independent coding protocol and reliability assessment, the expanded matrix, empirical identification/replication checks, and primary evidence for practical-harm claims. Distinguish faithful description, justified inference and true economic conclusions. Any of these could change the case or prevalence judgement without settling the formal validity of every test.
