# C002: Rational action without rejecting a zero null

**Status:** provisionally supported as a possibility, not a universal policy prescription. **Type:** project interpretation.

A decision rule need not require conventional statistical significance before preferring an action. This does not imply that uncertainty is irrelevant.

## Evidence trail

- [Manski 2019](../papers/manski-2019-treatment-choice.md), selected section 3.1.2: regret evaluation incorporates consequences as well as error probabilities.
- [Kandel–Stambaugh](../papers/kandel-stambaugh-1996-asset-allocation.md), abstract: model-based portfolio choice provides a concrete counterexample to a blanket rejection prerequisite.
- [Hoover–Siegler](../papers/hoover-siegler-2008-sound.md), pp. 18–19: objection to importing one actor's loss function into general scientific knowledge.

**Qualification:** a preferred action may change with priors, feasible states, costs, preferences or constraints. Model-implied utility is not independently demonstrated realised benefit. A significant finding can also fail to justify action when costs or risks dominate.

**Completed project test:** the [normal-prior illustration](../evidence/mathematical-checks.md) keeps data fixed and changes known adoption costs; posterior expected utility selects different actions despite the same non-significant zero-null result. This establishes a logical possibility under stated assumptions, not an actual benefit.

**New source trail:** Hoover–Siegler explicitly permits action on weak signals under asymmetric losses (p. 15). [Aradillas Fernández et al. 2026](../papers/aradillas-fernandez-etal-2026-robust-bayes.md), abstract, framework and conclusion, shows that ex ante and ex post robust Bayes rules can differ under a restricted partial-identification framework. This leaves the criterion itself a substantive choice. See the [critical synthesis](../../synthesis/critical-synthesis.md).
