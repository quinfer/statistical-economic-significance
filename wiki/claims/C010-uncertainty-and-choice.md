# C010: Replacing p-values supplies a complete answer

**Status:** rejected as a universal claim; task-specific procedures remain useful. **Type:** project interpretation.

Replacing a zero-null test with an effect size, an interval, an equivalence declaration or a decision criterion does not automatically remove uncertainty about analysis, identification or values.

## Evidence trail

- [Menkveld et al. 2024](../papers/menkveld-etal-2024-nonstandard-errors.md), introduction and II.D–III: different analysis paths on common data can yield dispersed results. We have not established equal validity of paths or a general standard-error correction.
- [Fitzgerald 2025](../papers/fitzgerald-2025-equivalence.md), pp. 22, 34–37: equivalence assessment needs margins; its wider interpretation is challenged in the [audit](../disagreements/equivalence-audit.md).
- [Aradillas Fernández et al. 2026](../papers/aradillas-fernandez-etal-2026-robust-bayes.md), sections 2–4: robust Bayes criteria can disagree in their restricted framework. This is a counterexample to uniqueness, not a reason to reject decision theory.

**What would narrow this conclusion:** a specified task with a defensible estimand, observation model, uncertainty set and agreed objectives can justify one procedure. Reporting those commitments is part of the solution. Merely replacing one summary statistic is not.
