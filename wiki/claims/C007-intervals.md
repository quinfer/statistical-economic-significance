# C007: Replacing p-values with intervals solves the problem

**Status:** the unqualified claim is rejected; communicative advantages remain plausible. **Type:** project interpretation.

## Evidence trail

The [2008 reply](../papers/mccloskey-ziliak-2008-reply.md), p. 50, itself recognises limits to an interval-only remedy. The [rejoinder](../papers/hoover-siegler-2008-rejoinder.md), pp. 60–61, emphasises links between tests and intervals. [Greenland et al.](../papers/greenland-et-al-2016.md), interval discussion, and [Morey et al.](../papers/morey-et-al-2016-intervals.md), abstract/introduction, expose interpretive issues from different perspectives.

**Project interpretation:** intervals can make magnitude comparisons easier without resolving identification, selection or competing goals. Replacing “excludes zero” with another binary badge can preserve the same habit. A compatibility reading requires attention to the underlying model; a posterior reading requires Bayesian assumptions.

**Next test:** work through Morey et al.'s examples and distinguish the mathematical relation between compatible tests and intervals from the empirical claim that one format improves research practice.

## Completed illustration

The [normal examples](../evidence/mathematical-checks.md) compare intervals with meaningful margins. They show that a precise zero estimate can support negligibility and a clearly nonzero estimate can remain uncertain relative to an economic threshold. This demonstrates logical distinctions, not that interval displays empirically improve behaviour. Complete readings of the reply p. 50 and rejoinder pp. 60–61 preserve agreement on the need for judgement and disagreement about test–interval equivalence as a response to communicative criticism.
