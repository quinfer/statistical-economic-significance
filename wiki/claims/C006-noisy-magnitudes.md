# C006: A large estimate is sufficient evidence of a large effect

**Status:** rejected as a universal proposition; qualifications need elaboration. **Type:** project interpretation.

The substantive size of a point estimate alone cannot certify its sign, magnitude or applicability to a target population. Selection and noise can undermine the apparent magnitude.

## Evidence trail

[Gelman–Carlin](../papers/gelman-carlin-2014.md), recommended design-analysis approach and discussion, supplies sign and exaggeration diagnostics. [Gelman–Stern](../papers/gelman-stern-2006.md), section 2, cautions against inferring differences from differing significance labels. [Hoenig–Heisey](../papers/hoenig-heisey-2001-power.md) warns against a common retrospective-power response.

**Critical qualification:** this does not make conventional significance a necessary or sufficient certification either. Precision, credible identification and substantive interpretation must be examined together. What constitutes enough precision depends on the claim and decision, not a universal standard-error ratio.

## Close-reading correction

The reply's p. 43 distinction between sampling precision and magnitude needs to distinguish the observed statistic from its unknown target. Conversely, *Sound and fury* contains local errors concerning limiting power (p. 14) and standardised statistics with little regressor variation (p. 17). Our [derivations](../evidence/mathematical-checks.md) correct those errors while preserving the argument that a noisy point estimate can mislead.
