# C011: Meaningful-effect certification can entail zero-null rejection

**Status:** established conditional mathematical implication, not a universal empirical rule. **Type:** project derivation.

For a positive fixed economic threshold δ, if an effect is certified by a one-sided lower confidence bound L>δ, then L>0. With a compatible monotone testing family and the same error level, rejecting H₀:β≤δ entails rejecting H₀:β≤0. This does not compare a one-sided test with an unrelated two-sided cutoff.

**Consequence:** the claim that conventional significance is never necessary under any interpretation of established economic significance is too broad. Under this particular operational definition, rejection of zero is logically implied. Rejection of zero alone is still insufficient to establish β>δ, and no separate preliminary test is required.

**Limits:** this is not a universal prerequisite for action, a Bayesian decision rule, equivalence, or importance of a known descriptive quantity. It assumes the chosen margin and inference procedure are defensible; model misspecification and weak identification can undermine their use.

## Evidence and checks

The [mathematical note](../evidence/mathematical-checks.md) proves the implication and gives an estimate that rejects zero but fails the meaningful-effect test. [Lakens 2017](../papers/lakens-2017-equivalence.md) motivates distinguishing zero, equivalence and meaningful-effect questions; the implication here is our derivation, not an attributed quotation.
