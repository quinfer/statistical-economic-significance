# Power, selection and magnitude errors

**Working distinction.** Power is a procedure's rejection probability at a specified alternative and design. It is not a probability that the observed estimate is true. A study's power is a function of effect size; there is no context-free power number.

**Source trail:** [Gelman–Carlin](../papers/gelman-carlin-2014.md) studies sign and exaggeration risks conditional on significance, using plausible effects external to the focal estimate. [Hoenig–Heisey](../papers/hoenig-heisey-2001-power.md) critiques post-experiment power interpretation. Their targets must be compared before treating these positions as contradictory. [Lakens 2022](../papers/lakens-2022-sample-size.md) links design justification to inferential goals.

**Project interpretation:** large observed effects selected from noisy studies or many searches can be especially poor guides to population magnitudes. More precision alone does not address biased designs or an invalid estimand. The [power and selection labs](../../simulations/README.md) now separate design-stage power from conditional selection. Their [evidence boundary](../evidence/simulation-labs.md) distinguishes synthetic operating characteristics from source findings; no same-data observed-power calculation is used to resolve uncertainty about a focal estimate.
