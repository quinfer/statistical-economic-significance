# Economic significance

**Project interpretation; working definition.** The phrase can refer to at least three different judgements: an effect would have consequential magnitude if true; evidence supports a consequential population effect; or a specified action has attractive consequences under uncertainty. These are not interchangeable.

Specify the estimand, units, population, horizon, scale, costs and distributional consequences before labelling an effect important. A standardised effect size or aggregate monetary total need not capture welfare. A decision threshold can depend on uncertain costs and values, not only the estimated effect.

**Evidence trail:** the [1996 critique](../papers/mccloskey-ziliak-1996.md) and [2008 reply](../papers/mccloskey-ziliak-2008-reply.md) motivate attention to substance; [Hoover–Siegler](../papers/hoover-siegler-2008-sound.md) challenges conclusions that bypass inferential reliability. The [book note](../papers/ziliak-mccloskey-2008-cult.md) now includes selected printed pp. 57–61 and 74–85; the [reading ledger](../evidence/cult-2008-reading.md) records the limits. The whole book has not been read.

**Open question:** what standard of support is needed for each kind of judgement? Consider descriptive population quantities and robust decisions as possible boundary cases to broad claims of dependence on sampling inference.

## Boundary cases now developed

The [substantive synthesis](../../synthesis/critical-synthesis.md) distinguishes four tasks: benchmark definition, observed finite-target description, unknown-effect inference, and action. [Checked examples](../evidence/mathematical-checks.md) show why strong evidence against zero may coexist with negligibility, and why acting without rejection is not proof of a large effect. A [nested meaningful-effect test](../claims/C011-nested-meaningful-tests.md) supplies a restricted counterexample to saying significance is never necessary.
