# Economic significance

**Illustration.** Suppose a lender regards a reduction of one percentage point in the one-year default rate as worthwhile for its eligible borrowers. A study estimates a reduction of 1.1 percentage points, with a standard error of 0.2 percentage points. The estimate exceeds the benchmark. What does the interval tell you that the estimate alone cannot?

Assume the study identifies the effect for these borrowers and gives an unbiased, normally distributed estimate with a known standard error. The 95% confidence interval is about 0.71 to 1.49 percentage points. It excludes zero and includes reductions on both sides of the lender’s benchmark. That leaves uncertainty about whether the programme clears the benchmark. To decide whether to adopt it, the lender also needs to weigh the costs of the programme and the consequences of the defaults it might prevent. ## What are you trying to establish?

**Project interpretation.** Keep three judgements separate when you describe a result as economically significant.

1. **What size of effect would matter?** Define the quantity you want to learn about, called the *estimand*. State its units, population and horizon. Explain how the scale of the decision, its costs and who gains or loses inform the benchmark. You can justify a benchmark before seeing the data.
2. **What does the study establish about that effect?** Compare the benchmark with the estimate and its uncertainty. Check that the study identifies the effect for the population you care about. If you only want to describe a fully observed quantity, such as last year’s recorded costs, sampling uncertainty may be irrelevant and measurement still matters. [Identification](identification.md) · [Confidence intervals](intervals.md)
3. **What should you do given what you know?** List the feasible actions and what each could cost or achieve. A lender might reasonably choose a small, reversible trial if its expected benefits justify the costs, even while the population effect remains uncertain. Spell out the assumptions behind that choice. [Decisions under uncertainty](decisions.md)

**Open question.** Suppose the programme costs twice as much as expected. Which judgement changes first? Would your answer depend on whether the goal is to report an effect, expand the programme or learn from a small trial?

- McCloskey and Ziliak argue for interpreting effect sizes in context. Hoover and Siegler ask what warrants confidence in those magnitudes. These examples qualify broad claims about when statistical significance is needed.
