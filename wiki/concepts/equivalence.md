# Equivalence and meaningful margins

**Source-grounded distinction:** [Schuirmann](../papers/schuirmann-1987-tost.md) and [Lakens](../papers/lakens-2017-equivalence.md) provide a route to assessing equivalence within bounds. Failure to reject an exact zero and rejection of effects outside a meaningful interval answer different questions.

For the standard continuous TOST construction using compatible one-sided tests at level alpha, equivalence corresponds to the associated two-sided 100(1−2alpha)% interval lying entirely within the prespecified bounds. Thus the familiar alpha = .05 construction uses a 90% interval. This does not turn a 90% interval into a posterior probability or require every equivalence procedure to use that construction.

**Project interpretation:** meaningful economic bounds need a substantive argument in natural units and sensitivity analysis. The [2018 tutorial](../papers/lakens-scheel-isager-2018.md) is relevant to prespecification. A wide fixed margin changes the substantive claim, without automatically invalidating nominal error control for that null. Selecting the margin in response to results adds a data-dependent inference problem. Economic inadequacy and statistical calibration must be assessed separately.

The [2025 economics working paper](../papers/fitzgerald-2025-equivalence.md) and [critical audit](../disagreements/equivalence-audit.md) deepen this distinction. An equivalence failure is inconclusive about whether a true effect lies outside the margin. Generic standardised benchmarks need not represent welfare thresholds, and independent respondents do not alone guarantee result-independent margin selection. A transparent elicitation protocol matters.

A one-sided viability question can require a one-sided meaningful threshold; a symmetric equivalence test answers a different question. [C011](../claims/C011-nested-meaningful-tests.md) and the [critical synthesis](../../synthesis/critical-synthesis.md) develop the implications.
