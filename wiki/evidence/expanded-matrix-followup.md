# Search for the expanded 187-paper AER matrix

**Status:** not recovered in this bounded public retrieval pass. **Researcher:** debate_sources; 24 October 2026. This is an access and provenance finding, not a finding that the matrix never existed or is unavailable everywhere. The [slide gate](../../reviews/review-gate.md) remains closed.

## Target and version boundary

**Source claim:** The directly read book pages acknowledge 50 omitted 1990s articles, credit Hoover and Siegler, and report an expanded corpus of 187 articles. Table 7.1 uses 182 + 187 = 369 articles across the two decades. Its reported 1990s Q16 affirmative rate is 20.9%, corresponding to the authors' roughly 79% adverse headline. See the [book reading ledger](cult-2008-reading.md), printed pp. 80–81, and [book note](../papers/ziliak-mccloskey-2008-cult.md). This follow-up relies on that documented direct reading; it does not claim another independent visual reading of the book.

The object sought was a version-identifiable, article-level 187-row matrix with the 19 questionnaire items, applicability/missingness information and an article list or crosswalk identifying the 50 additions. A published percentage table, review, lecture, article abstract or older rankings sheet does not supply those records.

**Verified prior material:** The [original coding audit](aer-coding-audit.md) retrieved 137 article rows with 19 binary items from Hoover's author-hosted Excel HTML export, attributed there to Stephen Ziliak's personal communication. Alphabetical and ranked versions agree after reordering. Those data reproduce the earlier 112/137 adverse Q16 count (81.75%), but they do not reproduce the expanded survey. Their exact versions and checksums remain in the [original manifest](../../sources/aer-audit/coding-manifest.json).

## Useful recovery that does not supply the expanded data

The author articles index links [Article_190.pdf](https://www.deirdremccloskey.com/docs/pdf/Article_190.pdf). Its first page was visually checked: **Stephen T. Ziliak and Deirdre N. McCloskey, “Size matters: the standard error of regressions in the American Economic Review,” The Journal of Socio-Economics 33 (2004), 527–546**, DOI `10.1016/j.socec.2004.09.024`. The abstract explicitly reports 137 articles. The downloaded object is a 20-page scan; attempted extraction of its first and last pages produced no text.

**Reading boundary:** This follow-up read the first page/abstract only and passed the scan to the root researcher for a separate final-version audit. It does not claim complete reading or table verification. The recovery establishes access to the final article, while leaving the expanded 2008 matrix unresolved. Root subsequently read selected final pages and independently checked tables with another agent; that later coverage is in the [final JSE ledger](jse-2004-version-check.md). It should not be conflated with the previously audited *Econ Journal Watch* version or treated as a second independent study.

## What remains unresolved

**Project interpretation:** The book's reported expansion cannot yet be independently reconstructed. None of this pass's retrieved material identifies all 187 articles with their item codes, the 50 additions, changes to the original 137 codes, or applicable denominators at item level. Consequently, this pass cannot verify the revised 79% headline, reproduce revised cross-tabulations, or assess whether inclusion changes the coding diagnosis. It neither confirms nor disproves the authors' claim that their main conclusions survive expansion.

The older Hoover–Siegler omission list is a different object. Its categories and disputed overlap are documented in the original audit; a list of alleged omissions does not identify which 50 were included in the book or show their new codes. No arbitrary subset was selected to manufacture a crosswalk. Likewise, rounded published marginals cannot determine the 187 × 19 joint coding matrix, especially where applicability varies. No synthetic or inferred article rows were created.

The minimum resolving package is a versioned article roster with stable bibliographic identifiers; the 19 responses with explicit non-applicability rules; the additional-50 crosswalk; any revisions to earlier codes; and the coding protocol or passage-level justifications. With that package, arithmetic, corpus reconciliation and selected independent recoding would become distinct feasible checks. Arithmetic agreement alone would still not validate the coding construct or establish inter-rater reliability.

No private files or authenticated author data were accessed, and no external correspondence was sent. Further public or author-supplied material may resolve the gap. Until then, retain the book's expanded percentages as **attributed reported results**, alongside the separately reproduced older 137-paper aggregates.
