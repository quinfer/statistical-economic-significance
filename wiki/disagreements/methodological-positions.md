# Strongest competing methodological positions

**Status: initial analytical reconstruction, not an adjudicated synthesis.** These positions overlap along different axes: scientific reporting labels, discovery standards and operational decision rules. No author is assigned an inference-free view merely because they criticise significance testing.

| Position | Strongest defensible argument | Main challenge | Starting evidence |

| Substantive magnitude and judgement | A zero-null threshold cannot tell us what matters scientifically or economically; interpretation needs meaningful alternatives and context | How should uncertain estimates support magnitude claims, and whose standards of importance apply? | [2008 reply](../papers/mccloskey-ziliak-2008-reply.md); [C001](../claims/C001-independence.md) |

## Where the disputes really lie

The original exchange contains both an empirical dispute over the diagnosis of economics practice and a methodological dispute over what testing contributes. The former needs an article-level coding audit; the latter needs specified inferential tasks and counterexamples. [C004](../claims/C004-practice-and-validity.md) keeps them separate.

Interval advocates and test defenders can disagree about interpretation or communication even where their procedures are mathematically connected. The source trail in [C007](../claims/C007-intervals.md) is therefore not a choice between “statistics” and “effect sizes”.

Finance raises an additional disagreement about what should count as replication and which model combines evidence appropriately. A balanced reading must include the optimistic hierarchical account alongside multiplicity concerns. [C008](../claims/C008-finance-replication.md) sets out the required comparison.

## Initial common ground to test

The consulted sources provide substantial grounds to distinguish statistical significance from importance and to reject mechanical interpretation. They do not establish agreement on a replacement, an optimal evidential threshold, or the correctness of particular policy decisions. Historical claims, prevalence estimates and alleged economic harms remain unadjudicated.
