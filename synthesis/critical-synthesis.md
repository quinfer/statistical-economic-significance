# Statistical and economic significance: evidence, importance and action

**Status:** substantive working synthesis, 24 October 2026; bounded by the reading record below and subject to critical review. **Slide gate: CLOSED by the user's instruction.** The [initial synthesis review](../reviews/2026-10-24-synthesis-review.md), [book/AER review](../reviews/2026-10-24-book-aer-review.md) and [final-source review](../reviews/2026-10-24-final-source-review.md) record objections and revisions; they do not authorise slides.

**Project conclusion.** Economic importance can be defined without a significance test. An unknown population or causal effect cannot generally be established as economically important merely by interpreting a point estimate: uncertainty and identification still need a defensible account. A rational action can nevertheless be selected without rejecting a zero null, because a choice under uncertainty is different from an evidential claim about an effect. This conclusion rejects both an inference-free assessment of unknown population or causal effects and a universal significance prerequisite for action. It also admits a narrower case in which a meaningful-effect test logically entails rejection of zero.

This is a conditional methodological conclusion, not a verdict on which authors damaged or improved economics. Its main primary-text foundation is the complete 2008 exchange: [Sound and fury](../wiki/papers/hoover-siegler-2008-sound.md), [reply](../wiki/papers/mccloskey-ziliak-2008-reply.md), and [rejoinder](../wiki/papers/hoover-siegler-2008-rejoinder.md). Selected sections of three recent additions extend the argument. The [35-source catalogue](../sources/literature-map.md) includes unread or partially read works; inclusion does not make all of them substantive evidence. Direct book reading now covers printed pp. 57–61 and 74–85 through an authorised preview. A bounded audit checks the earlier survey materials and five disputed AER cases. User-supplied final Darby and Bernheim–Wantz articles now permit direct checking of the two remaining final-text disputes; their reading depths and replication limits are separately recorded. The [book ledger](../wiki/evidence/cult-2008-reading.md), [coding audit](../wiki/evidence/aer-coding-audit.md) and case notes disclose those boundaries. The user-supplied PDF is a [2012 lecture](../wiki/evidence/ziliak-2012-lecture.md), not the book. Historical and medical harm claims and underlying empirical data remain unaudited.

## 1. What does “establish economic significance” mean?

**Project framework.** At least four claims must be distinguished.

| Claim | What must be justified | Role of a conventional zero-null test |
|---|---|---|
| A magnitude would matter | Units, population, horizon, alternatives, costs, preferences and distribution | Cannot supply those criteria |
| An observed accounting quantity matters | Adequate measurement and an appropriate descriptive target | Sampling inference may be unnecessary for a completely observed finite target |
| An unknown effect is materially large or small | Identification, observation model, evidence about magnitude and a relevant benchmark | One possible evidential component; neither a general substitute nor a universal prerequisite |
| An action is preferable | Feasible actions, uncertainty, consequences and a decision criterion | Relevant only insofar as it helps this decision problem |

For example, saying that a one-percentage-point reduction in default would matter to a lender defines a benchmark. Saying a policy causes that reduction requires evidence about an unknown counterfactual. Saying the lender should adopt a policy adds costs, risk, constraints and an evaluation rule. The same coefficient may imply different decisions for different lenders. A welfare criterion may also need distributional information that an average coefficient conceals.

“Inference” here means reasoning from observations and assumptions to an uncertain target beyond what is directly observed; it does not mean only p-values. This leaves genuine exceptions. An exact finite-population total may answer a descriptive question without sampling extrapolation. A deterministic accounting identity can transform known inputs. Such cases do not establish a general route from a noisy treatment-effect estimate to an unknown population magnitude. Measurement error and extrapolation can also reintroduce uncertainty even where sampling error is absent. See [C001](../wiki/claims/C001-independence.md) and [identification](../wiki/concepts/identification.md).

## 2. The strongest positions in the 2008 exchange

**Source claims.** Hoover and Siegler distinguish importance from precision and explicitly permit responding to weak signals when losses are asymmetric (*Sound and fury*, pp. 2, 15). McCloskey and Ziliak allow considered p levels with power when sampling precision is relevant (reply, p. 43), and reject reducing all losses to money (p. 52). The rejoinder asks whose losses should govern shared scientific knowledge (p. 59). These concessions rule out portraying the exchange as a simple choice between mechanical testing and wholesale refusal of statistical reasoning. The [matched ledger](../wiki/disagreements/2008-exchange.md) gives the page-level trail.

**Project reconstruction.** The strongest contextualist position is that evidence cannot determine its own economic importance or its acceptable error costs. The strongest inferential position is that interpretation cannot make a noisy estimate reliable, and that formal uncertainty assessment has uses beyond choosing one immediate action. Both positions withstand the weakest version of the opposing argument.

The remaining disagreements are substantial. The reply's categorical separation of sampling precision from magnitude on p. 43 is defensible for the numerical value of an estimate, but not generally for evidence about its unknown target. Under β̂∼N(β,s²), holding β̂ fixed and changing s changes the likelihood over β. The reply's one-realisation argument for time series (pp. 49–50) also does not establish that a process contains only one informative observation: information depends on the stochastic structure. Conversely, imposing a convenient stochastic model does not validate dependence assumptions or identify a causal effect.

Hoover and Siegler's defence needs scrutiny too. Their substantive formulation on p. 25 of *Sound and fury* risks treating non-rejection as necessarily uninformative imprecision and significance as a prerequisite for discussing magnitude. That sits uneasily with their earlier concessions; the precise-negligibility example below demonstrates the problem. *Sound and fury*, p. 14, gives an incorrect fixed-size power limit: for a continuous normal test, power approaches α near the null, not zero. Page 17 conflates a vanishing noncentrality with a realised standardised statistic becoming near zero. The rejoinder's p. 58 p-value parenthesis conflates a tail probability with Type I error. These local errors do not erase the noisy-estimate objection. They prevent treating the counterargument as technically beyond challenge. The [mathematical note](../wiki/evidence/mathematical-checks.md) provides derivations; the [ASA statement](../wiki/papers/wasserstein-lazar-2016.md), principles 1–2 and 5, provides the p-value/importance distinction.

A further scope correction matters: the rejoinder, p. 65, says the book was unavailable when the authors wrote their article and rejoinder. This exchange cannot stand in for a direct review of the full book. The new documentary audit below goes beyond adversarial descriptions, but leaves construct validity, score weights, coding reliability and the expanded survey unresolved. Agreement on a conceptual distinction does not settle prevalence; evidence of misuse would not establish that every calibrated test is useless. See [C004](../wiki/claims/C004-practice-and-validity.md).

## 2A. What direct book and AER reading changes

**Source evidence — the book.** Chapter 4, especially p. 60, advocates attention to priors, power and substantive judgement, and an explicit balance between formal and applied reasoning. The claim that economic importance cannot be read off a test is therefore better supported than a blanket attribution that the authors reject inference. Their programme still needs a defensible way to move from observations to unknown effects; the insistence that values matter does not itself supply that method. The [direct-reading ledger](../wiki/evidence/cult-2008-reading.md) distinguishes this published evidence from a prepublication preface and the later lecture.

The book reports a correction and expansion of its empirical base. Page 80 acknowledges 50 omitted 1990s articles and reports an expanded 187-paper survey. Table 7.1, p. 81, gives 20.9% positive answers to Question 16, implying roughly 79% adverse responses. The supplied earlier 137-row matrix reproduces 112 adverse responses, or 81.75%, behind the earlier 82% headline. These are different versions. Reproducing the earlier arithmetic neither verifies the expanded result nor validates the coding construct.

**Project audit — three levels of validity.** The [coding audit](../wiki/evidence/aer-coding-audit.md) separates arithmetic, source provenance and measurement. Its checks reproduce every row total in the supplied 1990s matrix, and the Q7 × Q16 cross-tabulation printed in both 2004 publications. The [newly retrieved final JSE scan](../wiki/evidence/jse-2004-version-check.md) sharpens the discrepancy findings: Q14/Q15 remain reversed relative to the matrix in final Tables 1 and 3 (pp. 529, 532), while the 1980s Q7 percentage is 47.3%, consistent with the 1996 original. The 13.2% Q7 error occurs in the checked EJW table, not the final JSE table. The audit cannot locate the cause of the remaining reversal. The 1980s file supplies author-paper rows and totals, but no original item-level codes for reproducing the published classifications; an independent recoding requires original articles and a new protocol. Forty-two rows sum to 20 responses, an unexplained inconsistency rather than 42 proven independent article miscoding errors. No passage-level coding rationales or independent coder-agreement estimates were recovered.

The countercritique needs the same scrutiny. Hoover and Siegler's supplied omission list has 82 rows while their rejoinder says 81. Simon's 1989 article appears in both their omission list and the supplied scored list; the provenance does not explain the overlap. This weakens the precision of the omission allegation without showing that all omissions are imaginary or harmless. The difference between 71 regression-and-test omissions and the larger list reflects scope, and must not be conflated with that overlap. The book's acknowledgement of 50 additions also cannot, without an article-level crosswalk, resolve every contested omission.

**Project inference boundary.** The expanded matrix cannot be reconstructed from the book's marginal percentages. Even exact column totals would not identify which articles received which codes or how codes co-occur. Subtracting older counts would additionally assume unchanged eligibility, item meanings, applicable denominators and codes for all retained papers. None of those assumptions has been verified. The [expanded-source search](../wiki/evidence/expanded-matrix-followup.md) therefore leaves the book's prevalence claim attributed but unaudited. Failure to retrieve the matrix is not evidence that it never existed or that its conclusions are false.

**Source evidence — construct distinctions.** Book Table 6.1 reports 80.2% discussing coefficient sizes, while 29.7% consider more than significance decisive. Both can be true: reporting magnitude is different from using it in an argument. However, p. 78's claim that about 70% of the 70% confused articles fail even to report magnitudes is difficult to reconcile with the first percentage if the event and universe are the same. This remains an unresolved apparent numerical/construct inconsistency, not a silently repaired paraphrase. Likewise, pp. 82–83 define a simulation criterion that can fail when its coefficients were selected solely by significance. Simply finding a simulation does not settle that code.

**Project judgement — selected cases.** The case audit asks whether a criticism accurately describes a paper, whether its procedure is warranted, and whether its economic conclusion is true. These are three separate questions. The following judgements concern the first two; none replicates the estimates or supplies a corrected misuse rate.

| Case and direct evidence | What survives and what changes |
|---|---|
| [Darby 1984](../wiki/evidence/darby-final-followup.md): published article fully read; disputed pages independently checked | Final p. 311 translates a coefficient into approximately −0.46/+0.61-percentage-point annual log-growth adjustments and retains a control with t = 0.31. Page 315 gives an economic explanation of a statistically nonsignificant coefficient difference. This verifies the narrow H&S magnitude defence, while leaving the oil-price exclusion and uncertainty after model selection unresolved. H&S’s confidence-interval-containment defence is not a reported covariance-aware equality test. |
| [Woodbury–Spiegelman 1987](../wiki/evidence/aer-darby-woodbury.md): selected published text, Tables 7–8 | Economic analysis includes costs, displacement and a scenario assuming complete take-up with unchanged benefit reductions, which reverses the apparent fiscal attraction. Caution is not explained solely by p > .05. The claim that treatment affected only one subgroup goes beyond comparing within-group significance; H&S’s cited defence also cannot establish heterogeneity from those comparisons. Effect contrasts and uncertainty are needed; the unread Table 6 and detailed report may contain additional tests. |
| [Bernanke–Blinder 1992](../wiki/evidence/aer-macro-finance-addiction.md): published tables and empirical discussion | Tables are neither literally ranked nor starred, but prose does rank predictive performance using significance. Variance decompositions provide another substantive measure, with ordering and identification qualifications. Neither the literal no-stars defence nor a no-magnitude accusation captures the whole argument. |
| [Bernheim–Wantz 1995](../wiki/evidence/bernheim-wantz-final-followup.md): selected published empirical and concluding sections read | Final pp. 543–544 distinguish and interpret economic effects. Table 4’s star identifies an alternative test; its panel A reports magnitudes and standard errors, and n. 17 reports direct tests of differences. Woodbury–Spiegelman's benefit–cost example also sharpens the main research question. Testing a ratio against zero does not test fiscal break-even at one, and neither test automatically determines a policy choice. A trial estimate must be distinguished from a scaled programme whose participation and spillovers could differ. The relevant criticism should target the specific inferential or decision step, rather than infer economic indifference from caution or infer sound inference from a cost calculation.

**Revised assessment.** Direct final texts now establish that the disputed Darby and Bernheim–Wantz passages contain substantive magnitude reasoning, strengthening the earlier draft-based correction. In Bernheim–Wantz, the added magnitude panel and direct contrasts also rule out a criticism framed solely as comparing separate significance outcomes. The audit preserves narrower criticisms of filtering, non-rejection and subgroup interpretation. It also finds errors and incomplete defences on the opposing side. These adversarially selected cases cannot establish prevalence across the AER. The stronger surviving lesson is the need to connect a precise claim to its estimand, uncertainty and decision criterion. Errors in a survey do not turn an arbitrary zero-null cutoff into a measure of welfare; errors in a paper do not make every use of inference worthless.

## 3. Magnitude requires uncertainty, but uncertainty is not a star

**Project illustrations.** The following calculations assume a valid known-variance normal likelihood, fixed units and fixed, substantively justified margins. They are synthetic counterexamples, not estimates from published economics studies. [Code, assumptions and values](../wiki/evidence/mathematical-checks.md) make them reproducible.

An estimate of .1 with standard error .02 strongly rejects zero; its 95% interval is approximately [.0608,.1392]. If the negligible region is (−1,1), it is also compatible with a formal equivalence declaration. Rejecting zero is therefore insufficient for meaningful importance. In contrast, an estimate of 2 with standard error 2 has a 95% interval of approximately [−1.92,5.92]. Calling the point estimate large does not establish a positive effect exceeding 1. Yet an estimate of zero with standard error .001 can establish negligibility inside (−.01,.01), although its zero-null p-value is one. Non-rejection does not always mean economically uninformative imprecision.

These examples support reporting magnitude and uncertainty together. They do not imply that a confidence interval is the whole solution. Frequentist coverage is a property of a procedure under a model; the displayed interval is not automatically a probability distribution over the fixed parameter. Confidence intervals can reproduce the same binary ritual if readers only ask whether zero is inside. Margin comparison, identification, multiplicity, selection and model adequacy remain necessary considerations. [Morey et al.](../wiki/papers/morey-et-al-2016-intervals.md) and [C007](../wiki/claims/C007-intervals.md) supply the interpretation trail; [Gelman and Carlin](../wiki/papers/gelman-carlin-2014.md) connects low power to misleading selected magnitudes and signs.

There is an important qualification to a slogan that statistical significance is “never necessary.” If positive importance is operationalised as a lower confidence bound L exceeding a positive threshold δ, then L>δ implies L>0. Within the same compatible one-sided testing procedure and error level, rejection of H₀:β≤δ entails rejection of H₀:β≤0. This logical nesting does not require a separate preliminary zero-null test. It does mean that an unqualified denial of necessity is false under this particular definition of established positive importance. It says nothing by itself about rational action, and must not mix one-sided and two-sided cutoffs.

## 4. What the 2024–2026 update changes

The update is targeted, not an exhaustive systematic review. It adds three contributions to reach 35 core works and records other verified candidates and versions in the [search/disposition log](../sources/update-2024-2026.md). Selecting these three addresses distinct gaps; it is not a ranking of scholarly quality.

**Source claim:** [Menkveld et al. (2024)](../wiki/papers/menkveld-etal-2024-nonstandard-errors.md), abstract, introduction and sections II.D–III, studies variation across analysts using the same data and reports that peer feedback reduces dispersion. **Project implication:** a precise result under one chosen analysis need not capture uncertainty across defensible analyses. However, analyst dispersion is not automatically bias, a collection of equally valid estimands, or an independent variance component to add to every reported standard error. Publication selection may change both dispersion and bias. The [uncertainty note](../wiki/concepts/uncertainty-beyond-sampling.md) keeps these distinctions explicit.

**Source claim:** [Fitzgerald (2025)](../wiki/papers/fitzgerald-2025-equivalence.md), a working paper, reanalyses null claims using equivalence tests. It explicitly distinguishes failure to establish equivalence from evidence of a large relationship (p. 22), and generic literature-audit margins from margins suited to individual questions (p. 34). **Project implication:** this is directly relevant contemporary evidence about the support offered for negligibility claims. Its broader interpretation as revealing unacceptable Type II errors needs more information about the true effects. A synthetic population of exact-zero effects measured too imprecisely to pass a narrow equivalence test can have universal equivalence failure without any false zero nulls. The [adversarial audit](../wiki/disagreements/equivalence-audit.md) separates this objection from the case for testing negligibility directly.

A wide fixed equivalence margin changes the claim under test; it does not automatically invalidate nominal error control for that changed null. An economically inappropriate margin and a data-dependent margin are different problems. Likewise, symmetric equivalence is not identical to a one-sided net-benefit question. With positive outcome valuation V and adoption cost c, β<c/V implies negative net benefit. Requiring |β|<c/V additionally excludes large harmful effects, although those also make adoption unattractive. Economic reasoning must shape the tested hypothesis, and uncertainty about V or c must not disappear from the analysis.

**Source claim:** [Aradillas Fernández et al. (2026)](../wiki/papers/aradillas-fernandez-etal-2026-robust-bayes.md), abstract and conclusion, shows that ex ante and ex post robust Bayes criteria can yield different rules under partial identification; randomisation can be optimal under either. **Project implication:** replacing significance thresholds with decision theory does not deliver a uniquely preferred choice without further commitments. The results use a specified normal-signal framework, linear mappings, a convex symmetric parameter set and a restrictive prior class. They are not a theorem that all Bayesian criteria disagree or all decisions should be randomised. The authors leave the comparative merits of the criteria open. More data may reduce sampling uncertainty without resolving the gap between identified information and payoff-relevant quantities.

## 5. Decisions can proceed before an effect is established

**Project derivation.** Consider a synthetic policy with an unbiased normal estimate of £400 per person and known standard error £500. Specify a proper N(0,1000²) prior for its effect. The posterior mean is £320 and posterior standard deviation £447.21. The frequentist two-sided zero-null p-value is .4237. With known per-person adoption cost £100, zero payoff for the status quo and linear risk-neutral utility, posterior expected net benefit is £220. Adoption is preferred under those assumptions. With cost £500, the same data and prior favour the status quo. Neither choice certifies the true effect as positive or large.

The example fixes the action set and excludes delayed learning, irreversibility constraints, distributional objectives and uncertain costs. Altering the prior or losses can alter the answer. Its function is to disprove a universal rejection prerequisite, not to prescribe a real policy. [Manski (2019)](../wiki/papers/manski-2019-treatment-choice.md) directly develops the treatment-choice distinction; [Kandel and Stambaugh](../wiki/papers/kandel-stambaugh-1996-asset-allocation.md) supplies an investment connection at the more limited abstract reading depth recorded in its note.

Other decision frameworks need not use a single posterior. A minimax-regret procedure selects a data-dependent rule that minimises the maximum expected regret across feasible states, with each state’s expectation taken over its sampling distribution. Choosing an action after seeing the data is a different operation. Robust dominance can sometimes identify a preferred action without precise probabilities if it wins over every relevant state. But an uncertainty set has to be justified, and dominance across a poorly chosen set is weak assurance. Neither uncertainty nor welfare conflict is removed by changing vocabulary from inference to decisions.

**Recent counterposition, abstract-level evidence:** the verified final abstract of [Armstrong, Kitagawa and Tetenov (2026)](../sources/update-2024-2026.md) explicitly locates hypothesis testing, estimation and preanalysis plans within Wald’s decision framework. Thus testing and decision theory are not mutually exclusive camps. Whether a given test encodes a satisfactory decision problem is a further question; unread final details are not imported here.

This also clarifies the “whose loss?” challenge. Scientific reports intended for many users can expose estimates, uncertainty, design and assumptions without optimising one person's payoff. A particular investor or policymaker then combines that evidence with their constraints and objectives. A common evidential standard and heterogeneous decisions can coexist; choosing one universal substantive cutoff is not the only alternative to significance rituals.

## 6. Conditional judgement and what would change it

**Project judgement.** The strongest case against mechanical significance remains intact: a zero-null threshold cannot determine importance, negligibility or optimal action. The strongest case against ignoring inference also remains intact: a substantively impressive estimate does not become reliable by being translated into money, jobs or returns. Equivalence testing addresses a real inferential gap when margins are defensible; it does not supply their economic justification. Decision theory addresses action under uncertainty; it does not turn the chosen action into proof of the underlying magnitude.

This leaves specific unresolved work, not a presumption that every position is equally supported:

1. **The historical and prevalence verdict:** the bounded audit changes several case descriptions, but a complete corpus crosswalk, the expanded 187-paper matrix and passage-level recoding with reliability checks remain needed. The final Darby/Bernheim–Wantz access gaps are now resolved, but source reading is not data replication or an identification audit. The rest of the book, endnotes and primary historical/medical evidence require reading before a full-argument or practical-harm verdict.
2. **The current equivalence audit:** replicating the estimates, examining exclusions and dependence, and varying economically defensible margins could change the breadth of its practical diagnosis. Equivalence failures alone cannot identify the proportion of asserted negligible relationships that are actually outside their justified margins. That proportion also differs from a conventional zero-null test’s Type II error probability.
3. **The finance implication:** checking estimand comparability and the mechanism behind analyst dispersion could change how much weight to place on analysis uncertainty for a particular investment problem. Gross alpha is not automatically achievable net welfare or return.
4. **The decision-theoretic scope:** full proof and application review, broader prior classes and comparison with the final 2026 Armstrong–Kitagawa–Tetenov paper could alter claims about which criteria are useful in practice. That paper is a prioritised candidate, not silently treated as fully read evidence here.

The immediate research standard is consequently task-specific: state what magnitude would matter; state what the data and assumptions support; and state the additional premises needed for a decision. Preserve disagreement whenever those premises remain disputed. The [review gate](../reviews/review-gate.md) stays closed.
