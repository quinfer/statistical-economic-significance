# Initial literature taxonomy

**Status:** proposed, with an initial verified-identity corpus. Selection is purposive and argumentative, not a systematic review or an exhaustive search through October 2026. The [catalogue](../sources/literature-map.md) records reading depth separately.

The target is 25–35 distinct academic works, including books and methodological commentary. Coverage should be judged by the questions and opposing arguments represented, not equal numbers assigned to presumed camps.

| Strand | Question | Initial anchors | Inclusion rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Original economics debate | What is wrong with observed practice, and what follows for inference? | McCloskey–Ziliak 1996; Ziliak–McCloskey 2004 and 2008; all three papers in the 2008 exchange | Separate empirical audits of practice from logical and normative arguments |
| 2. Meaning and reform of inference | Interpret, replace, retain or tighten significance thresholds? | ASA 2016; Cohen 1994; Gelman–Stern 2006; Greenland et al. 2016; Benjamin et al. 2018; 2019 reform proposals; Imbens 2021 | Include abolition, stricter standards and qualified retention |
| 3. Precision, power and interval meaning | How reliable are magnitude estimates and absence claims? | Neyman 1937; Hoenig–Heisey 2001; Gelman–Carlin 2014; Morey et al. 2016; Lakens 2022 | Sampling variability, interpretation, design and selection are distinct issues |
| 4. Meaningful thresholds and equivalence | How can evidence rule out effects that would matter? | Schuirmann 1987; Lakens 2017; Lakens–Scheel–Isager 2018 | Direct bridge between a substantive margin and a statistical procedure |
| 5. Decisions and identification | What should an actor do before uncertainty is resolved? | Wald 1950; Manski 2019 and 2021; Deaton–Cartwright 2018 | Loss, regret, feasible states, causal interpretation and transportability |
| 6. Finance applications and contested replication | Does statistical weakness imply decision irrelevance or failure to replicate? | Kandel–Stambaugh 1996; Harvey–Liu–Zhu 2016; McLean–Pontiff 2016; Harvey–Liu 2020; Jensen–Kelly–Pedersen 2023 | Portfolio choice, multiple searches, return decay, missed discovery and an opposing replication account |

The machine-readable registry splits strand 3 between `intervals` and `power`, and strand 5 between `decision` and `identification`, to support more precise links. Papers may inform several strands even when assigned one catalogue theme.

## Boundaries and gaps

Include foundational work regardless of age and contemporary methodological work with direct relevance to economics or finance. Adjacent psychology and biostatistics sources provide methods, not automatic evidence about economics practice. Do not turn biomedical equivalence conventions or standardised psychological effect sizes into economic importance thresholds without a domain argument.

This first pass reaches 2023. A focused 2024–2026 update, forward/backward citation tracing, and coverage of partial identification, robust inference and welfare-based policy learning remain due before the corpus is treated as settled. Historical Fisher/Neyman–Pearson/Gosset disputes need primary texts if they become substantive claims. Do not enlarge the corpus merely to display those names.

For the next pass, prioritise gaps over adding redundant threshold manifestos. Consider replacing an overlapping commentary to make room for an independently verified economics paper on policy learning or partial identification. Keep the total within 25–35 unless the user revises the scope.

## Inclusion and exclusion decisions

Include a work when its identity is verified and it contributes an essential opposing argument, method or application. Preserve unread classics as acquisition priorities, with no attributed substantive claims. Include direct replies because the disagreement itself is part of the research object.

Do not count the 2004 *Size matters* versions twice. Do not count Manski's 2019 draft under its old title as an additional 2021 publication. Record candidates not retained, inaccessible material and unresolved metadata in the [search log](../sources/search-log.md).

## Update to coverage

The [2024–2026 pass](../sources/update-2024-2026.md) adds analyst variation, economics-specific equivalence and robust decisions under partial identification. These deepen existing strands rather than creating a separate prestige-based list. The 35-work cap leaves other verified candidates, including Armstrong–Kitagawa–Tetenov and Mitton, in a prioritised reading queue. Publication status and online/issue dates remain explicit.
